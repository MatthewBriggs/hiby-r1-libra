## Scanner: why tracks go missing

The stock scanner's path field is **256 bytes**. Measured against the card:
every indexed file has a path of 255 characters or fewer; every music file it
missed is 256 or more. There is no overlap, so this is a hard buffer limit, not
a corruption or a timing problem — 76 tracks across the long-titled classical
sets were simply never seen, and "Update database" would not have helped.

The other 99 files it skips are `._` AppleDouble sidecars left by copying on a
Mac. Skipping those is correct; this app skips them too.

**Since fixed by renaming:** 94 files clipped, over-long files down from 76 to
**24**. The remaining 24 are over the limit because of their *folder* paths, so
the filename alone cannot save them. The mapping is at
`hiby-fw/restore-point/rename_plan_20260801.tsv` and every rename is reversible.

**Still open:** the index holds the 94 files under their old names, and those
rows are dead until a real rescan. The app skips rows whose file has gone and
the per-album folder sweep picks the files up under their new names, so albums
look right — but by sweep, not by index.

(b)-(e) below are about system libraries/daemons, not this app's own compile target, and are unaffected by this finding -- kept for reference. **Correction to this closeout's own count, 2026-08-27: five system libraries, not two.** Rechecked by grepping every `dlopen()` call site rather than recalling them -- `libasound.so.2` (`audio.c`), `libjpeg.so.9` (`cover.c`), `libfdk-aac.so.2` (`aac.c`), `libopus.so` (`opus_dec.c`) and `libvorbis.so` (`vorbis_dec.c`). All five are stock glibc-built rootfs files reached the same way, so the finding above holds harder rather than less: a static musl build loses every one of them, which is all audio output, all cover art, and the AAC/M4A, Opus and Vorbis format coverage on top. **Corroborated independently** -- `Starnished66/R1-open-source-player` hit the identical wall from the opposite direction: its own notes record planning to `dlopen()` this device's system `libfdk-aac.so.2` and finding it impossible because musl's `dlopen()` refuses to work from statically-linked binaries (confirmed by them via QEMU against the real device library), which is why they vendor FAAD2 instead and why that project carries a GPL obligation it would otherwise not have. **If musl is ever revisited, every replacement is substitution, not a linkage trick**: `tinyalsa` (github.com/tinyalsa/tinyalsa) for local output, a vendored AAC decoder, vendored Opus/Vorbis (we already carry their headers and `ogg/framing.c`), and a vendored IJG jpeg9 per (b). One architectural cost comes with `tinyalsa` specifically and is worth knowing before rather than after: it talks **only** to numbered `hw:` cards and implements no ALSA plugin layer at all, so it cannot reach `bluealsa` -- our whole Bluetooth path (`bluealsa:PROFILE=a2dp` with a `plug` fallback in `pcm_open()`, plus `amixer -D bluealsa` for volume) is unreachable through it. That project's own answer, from `audio_output.c`: keep tinyalsa for local hardware, and route Bluetooth and USB through a spawned `aplay -D bluealsa` subprocess with PCM piped into it, since `-D` resolves through the real ALSA rather than tinyalsa. Their header notes that path accumulated several real-device Bluetooth bugs one at a time, so adopting it means re-earning fixes we already hold (BG67/BG79's mixer re-push, format negotiation, underrun recovery). Why it works under musl at all is the transferable part: a **subprocess** boundary is safe across libc implementations -- a musl process may freely spawn a glibc binary -- which is precisely the property `dlopen()` lacks, so "shell out to it" stays viable everywhere "load it into our own address space" does not. | Only matters once `hiby_player` is genuinely replaced, not for today's LD_PRELOAD build — that build shares one process (and one heap, one TLS layout) with `hiby_player`'s own glibc, and mixing glibc and musl inside a single process is unsafe, not just untested. `zig cc` (already the toolchain, see `build.sh`) has musl MIPS targets built in, arguably less fiddly than the current glibc-2.22 version pin. Candidates, split by who actually controls the source: **(a) our own binary** — `music_hook.c` + everything `build.sh` compiles into `libmusic_hook.so`/`library_standalone` (including the vendored single-file libs: `dr_flac`/`dr_mp3`/`dr_wav`, `ogg`/`vorbis`/`opus`, `sqlite3.c`, `miniz` as of R33) — moves to musl automatically the day the standalone build's target triple changes; no separate action needed. **(b) libjpeg** — **correction, 2026-08-24: the "borrows from hiby_player" framing below was wrong**, caught rereading `cover.c` directly rather than from memory. `dlopen("libjpeg.so.9", ...)` (falling back to `/usr/lib/libjpeg.so.9`) is a plain by-path load our own process makes for itself -- `dlopen()` has no mechanism to reach into another process's address space regardless, so there was never a real "shortcut" to lose. Confirmed working standalone the same way it always did: `/usr/lib/libjpeg.so.9` is a rootfs-shipped file, loaded independently by whichever process calls `dlopen()` on it, with or without `hiby_player` ever running. Net: real savings, but they scale with how much of the stack gets taken over — (a) is free once standalone is committed to, (b) is a bounded one-time cost, (c) is its own separate project that trades working infrastructure for maintenance burden and shouldn't be bundled into "replace `hiby_player`" scope casually. |

So: **ADB** = `/usr/bin/adbon` + `NormalDRP`; **Storage** = `/usr/bin/adboff` + `NormalDRP` (that script starts mass storage itself); **OTG** = `S440adb stop` + `usb_dev_mass_storage.sh stop /dev/mmcblk0` + `Source` (note `adboff` is wrong here -- it *starts* mass storage, which OTG must not have). `st_usb_mode()` reads live gadget-bind state from configfs rather than a cached flag, so it needs a third case: neither gadget bound **and** `tcs1421_cfg` reading `Source` = OTG. **Prerequisite for OTG to be useful**: a kernel with `CONFIG_SND_USB`/`CONFIG_SND_USB_AUDIO`. Verified live that without it a connected USB DAC visibly charges (VBUS flows, host role engages) but never enumerates, because there is no `snd-usb-audio` to bind -- it looks "not seen". Built as `USBAUDIO_1`/`USBAUDIO_2` in the kernel repo; the stock kernel's status is unconfirmed. **Design constraint, learned the hard way**: entering OTG kills adb outright, because the board stops being a USB device. The on-screen UI still works, so switching back via Settings is fine -- but this mode must **never** be applied automatically at boot before the UI is up, and ideally should not persist across a reboot at all. A boot-time OTG apply cost a full lockout during investigation: `/usr/data` survives firmware flashes by design, so a bad setting there could not be cleared by reflashing, and with adb gone there was no way in. If it is persisted, pair it with an escape hatch on the SD card (a marker file the supervisor checks) since the card is the only surface reachable without a shell. Related: RP5 (misc system settings), and `docs/08`/`patches/0006` in the kernel repo for the separate VBUS-output question. |

## Inspiration

Ideas taken from other R1 player projects. Sourced by **reading their code for technique only** -- every project below is GPLv3 and this one is MIT, so nothing here is a port: the mechanism, register, or ordering is the takeaway, the implementation is ours. Same approach already used for `open_hiby_player` (see `NOTES-standalone-player-analysis.md`) and for RP1/RP3's corroboration.

Current source: `github.com/Starnished66/R1-open-source-player` at tag `Beta-1` (commit `7d0b92d`, analysed 2026-08-27). Its own `BATTERY_REPORT.md` is worth reading before trusting any battery claim from that project -- including its release notes' "battery life is now on par with Stock", which that report itself explicitly declines to support (27-minute window, 1% gauge resolution, taken right after a large gauge recalibration, mixed-radio workload). The mechanisms below stand on their own; the headline number does not.

**Not on this list, deliberately: suspend-to-RAM.** They ship it (`power_suspend.c`) with the same `bt_suspend` -> `fb0/blank=4` -> `/sys/power/state=mem` -> unblank sequence `deep_suspend()` already independently arrived at, plus one thing we don't do (BlueZ D-Bus adapter power-off *before* the raw `bt_suspend` UART teardown, because suspending with a live A2DP link reportedly reboots the device via watchdog -- a different failure mode than the recoverable link-drop `suspend_ok()`'s own comment anticipated). That is a real hazard, but in **dormant** code: the "Idle sleep" Settings row was removed, `deep_sleep_enabled` defaults to 0, and nothing reaches `deep_suspend()` without a hand-edited `music.conf`. Suspend stays withdrawn on its own evidence -- an unattended overnight run needed a hard power cycle and measured no real improvement -- which their release does not overturn. One thing has changed since that decision: it was paused "pending source" for the SDIO teardown worry (WiFi and the SD card share that bus), and source for both reference projects now exists, so that specific question is finally checkable if suspend is ever reopened. Not a reason to reopen it.

| # | Item | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| INS1 | **Screen-off audio low-power mode (bigger decode chunk)** | Cheapest item here by a distance. They double the decode chunk from 4096 to 8192 frames whenever the screen is off (`audio.c`'s `NORMAL_CHUNK_FRAMES`/`LOW_POWER_CHUNK_FRAMES`, flipped by `audio_set_low_power_mode()`, called from the same `screen_on_now != screen_was_on` edge that already toggles their input layer). Halves wakeups per second of audio, which is a race-to-idle win rather than a throughput one -- worth having even though our own decode CPU is already low (3-13% of one core on the Bluetooth path per `audio.c`'s own `apply_decode_priority()` comment), since the saving is in how often the SoC is woken, not how much work it does once awake. Note their buffers are sized to `MAX_CHUNK_FRAMES` (the larger of the two) at allocation time so the switch is just a read-size change, never a realloc -- worth copying that shape rather than allocating per mode. Ours would hook the same place the existing lock/screen-off handling already runs. |
| INS2 | **Dim the backlight before turning it off** | They dim to 40% of the user's configured brightness after 10s of inactivity (`SCREEN_DIM_AFTER_MS`, `backlight_set_dimmed()`), separately from and well before the full screen-off timeout. Their stated reasoning is simply that the panel dominates power while lit, and 40% is "visibly dimmer without making low user settings unreadable". We have brightness control and a screen-off path but no intermediate dim tier. Two details worth taking with it: the dim level is a percentage *of the user's own setting* (not an absolute), so someone already running dark doesn't get dimmed into unreadability; and it restores the exact prior brightness rather than a fixed default. Interacts with BG95 (brightness resetting on sleep/wake) -- check that fix's own restore path before adding a second thing that writes brightness. |
| INS3 | **85% charge limiter via the AXP2101 PMIC** | Biggest genuinely-new capability here, and the one where reading their work saves the most: `charge_limiter.h` documents **three** register-level attempts, two of them dead ends we would otherwise repeat. (1) `/sys/class/power_supply/usb/charge_control_limit_max` is wired to the *wrong* register -- it lands on REG16, the input-current limit (how much the USB port may supply), not battery charge current. (2) REG62 (ICC, fast-charge current) is the right register but the wrong mechanism: it only constrains the constant-CURRENT phase, and the charger enters constant-VOLTAGE well before 85% on this battery, so zeroing it stopped charging at ~91-92%, not the configured target. (3) What actually works: `chg_en`, bit 1 of `module_en`/REG18H, i2c bus 0 address 0x34 -- disables the whole charger state machine, CC and CV alike. **Must be read-modify-write**: that same register holds `gauge_en` and `watchdog_en`. Needs a discharge hysteresis window or noisy 84/85% readings restart the charger repeatedly and overshoot. Two hard-won negative results come with it: `axp_battery/current_now` is **frozen** (a static passthrough of a configured maximum, not an ADC reading) so no software wattage measurement is possible on this device at all; and the only trustworthy live charge state is REG01 bits[2:0] straight from the PMIC -- both `battery/status` and `usb/current_now` were caught reporting stale/wrong values during their investigation. They also expose a separate 500mA safe-charging cap. Ours would need its own i2c access; nothing in this codebase talks to the PMIC today, so this is the largest of the four. |
| INS4 | **Throttle battery/status sysfs reads** | Housekeeping-grade, but free. They cache battery capacity/status behind a 5s TTL (`BATTERY_CACHE_TTL_MS`, `battery.c`) rather than reading sysfs per call, add display smoothing on top so the shown percentage doesn't jitter with the gauge (`BATTERY_DISPLAY_STABLE_MS` 15s, `BATTERY_DISPLAY_STEP_MS` 60s, plus a jump threshold above which a gap is treated as a real change rather than noise), and drop visible status polling from every 500ms tick to every 2s -- their own comment notes that last one alone "removes three quarters of those reads". Worth auditing what we poll per tick while the screen is on before copying any specific number; the principle (cache with a TTL, smooth the display separately from the read) is the transferable part. Also note their rediscovery-on-failure detail: a driver can disappear and reappear across suspend, so a failed read clears the cached sysfs device name rather than pinning a stale one forever. |

| H9 | Nero-style `chpl` chapters in `.m4b` are not read | `mp4_chapters()` finds chapters exactly one way: the QuickTime text track, picked by its `hdlr` handler type being literally `text` (`mp4.c`).
